# Lesson 1: Start with the story and the delivery

Course: Aerial Photography and Videography

Status: DRAFT. Original SkyScholar authoring; technical and instructional review pending.

Estimated study time: 20-30 minutes including the desk exercise (author estimate).

## Learning focus

Translate a client brief into a shot list.

## Lesson

A property client requests a short video showing the entrance, the relationship between buildings and the surrounding landscape. The useful first deliverable is a shot list. Each shot should answer a viewer question. An entrance overview establishes access; a wider view establishes location; a detail provides texture. Repeating the same view at several heights does not necessarily add information.

Specify the intended format before capture: orientation, duration, resolution, frame rate and delivery channel. These choices affect framing and editing. Keep a record of the client's priorities and any privacy restrictions. A request for an attractive result does not supply permission to film every neighbouring property or person.

Exposure decisions involve aperture where adjustable, shutter time and sensitivity. A longer exposure can introduce motion blur; a shorter one can make movement look more discrete. Increasing sensitivity may add visible noise. The appropriate compromise depends on the image and motion required. Avoid prescribing one setting for every aircraft, lens, light level and output.

Continuity is a planning problem. Adjacent shots should relate in direction, subject position and light. Prepare a sequence that can be edited without needing risky extra passes to repair missing coverage. Keep the original files, work from copies and record any transformations used in post-production.

For this lesson, plan with still images or a simulator. Evaluate whether every proposed shot advances the story and whether the finished edit can be delivered within the agreed scope. The grading criterion is deliberate communication, not the closeness of the aircraft to an obstacle.

## Desk exercise

Produce a six-shot storyboard for a fictional property without flying. Include shot purpose, duration, framing, privacy consideration and delivery format.

## Marking guide

Each shot should have a distinct purpose, a coherent sequence and a feasible delivery specification. Deduct for duplicated coverage presented as a complete story.

## Reflection

Name one assumption you would verify before applying this lesson to a real project. Identify the source or competent person who could resolve it.
