# Lesson 1: Professional judgement includes what you choose not to collect

Course: Drone Ethics and Professionalism

Status: DRAFT. Original SkyScholar authoring; technical and instructional review pending.

Estimated study time: 20-30 minutes including the desk exercise (author estimate).

## Learning focus

Identify affected stakeholders and information harms.

## Lesson

A construction update can unintentionally capture workers, neighbouring homes or vehicle registrations. The fact that a sensor can record detail does not mean every detail belongs in the client's report or a public portfolio. Start by identifying who may be affected and why the information is needed.

Define the minimum useful data for the agreed purpose. Consider framing, timing, output resolution, access controls and retention. These are practical design choices. Relevant legal requirements and contractual permissions must be checked for the jurisdiction and project; an ethics checklist does not replace that review.

Truthful reporting is another part of professional conduct. State whether a finding is observed, inferred or unverified. Do not remove a limitation because a client prefers a more confident conclusion. A clear image can support a narrow observation while leaving the cause uncertain. If an error is discovered, retain a record of the correction and tell affected recipients which version supersedes the earlier one.

Respect the boundary of your competence. A pilot or image analyst should not imply a professional certification or specialist opinion that has not been provided. Attribute contributions and preserve permitted source provenance. Obtain explicit approval before using client material in marketing.

For this lesson, evaluate a fictional publication request. Explain the purpose, affected people, minimum data, permission gaps and a safe alternative. A professional response should preserve the legitimate client objective while making responsibilities and uncertainty clear.

## Desk exercise

Draft a response to a fictional client asking to publish an unredacted site image. Include an alternative and a version-correction process.

## Marking guide

The response should identify stakeholders, minimise data, avoid invented consent and propose a useful alternative with a clear approval boundary.

## Reflection

Name one assumption you would verify before applying this lesson to a real project. Identify the source or competent person who could resolve it.
