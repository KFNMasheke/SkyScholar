# Lesson 1: A longer route requires a different assurance argument

Course: BVLOS Operations

Status: DRAFT. Original SkyScholar authoring; technical and instructional review pending.

Estimated study time: 20-30 minutes including the desk exercise (author estimate).

## Learning focus

Explain how losing direct visual observation changes safety assumptions.

## Lesson

This is a conceptual, desk-based introduction. It does not authorise BVLOS operations or provide a flight procedure. Work with a fictional mission and keep all approval, aircraft capability and crew qualification questions unresolved until supported by the appropriate authority and competent specialists.

In a basic visual operation, direct observation helps the crew understand aircraft position and surrounding hazards. A concept that removes or changes that observation must explain how its safety functions will be achieved and assured. Extending a route on a planning screen does not supply that assurance.

Describe the intended operation before selecting technology. State the area, timing, aircraft, crew roles, population exposure and airspace context. Then identify the functions needed: command and control, position awareness, monitoring, containment, contingency management and coordination. For each function, ask what happens when it degrades and what evidence supports the proposed response.

A redundancy claim needs careful scrutiny. Two components that depend on the same power source, network or environmental assumption may share a failure mode. Record independence assumptions and ask a competent reviewer to challenge them. Avoid presenting a consumer connectivity indicator as proof of an approved command-link performance level.

The output of this lesson is a concept of operations with a list of unanswered assurance questions. It should make the evidence gap easier to review. Later modules require jurisdiction-specific approval references and expert instruction before any operational training is released.

## Desk exercise

Write a fictional BVLOS concept with five safety functions, a failure question for each, and named evidence required before approval review.

## Marking guide

Assess quality of questions and traceability, not operational readiness. A passing desk exercise must not be treated as authorisation to fly.

## Reflection

Name one assumption you would verify before applying this lesson to a real project. Identify the source or competent person who could resolve it.
