# Lesson 1: Understand the motion before using the controls

Course: Drone Flight Fundamentals

Status: DRAFT. Original SkyScholar authoring; technical and instructional review pending.

Estimated study time: 20-30 minutes including the desk exercise (author estimate).

## Learning focus

Describe aircraft axes and basic motion.

## Lesson

Use a simulator for this lesson. Keep propellers removed from any aircraft used only for desk familiarisation. The purpose is to connect control input, aircraft attitude and movement, not to teach an unsupervised first flight.

An aircraft has longitudinal, lateral and vertical axes. Roll, pitch and yaw describe rotation about those axes. Position describes where the aircraft is; attitude describes its orientation. They are related but different. A multirotor can change its attitude before a clear positional change becomes visible, so a pilot who reacts only to displacement may respond late.

In a simplified stationary hover, the upward component of thrust balances weight. Tilting the thrust vector introduces a horizontal component. If total thrust does not increase, its vertical component becomes smaller. This explains why a demand for horizontal acceleration also needs performance margin to maintain height. It does not provide a universal control setting: flight controllers and aircraft designs implement control differently.

Orientation changes the pilot's visual interpretation. A simulated aircraft facing the pilot makes a leftward aircraft motion appear on the opposite side of the pilot's viewpoint. Start by describing the aircraft's own orientation aloud. Avoid guessing from the screen location alone. A camera view and a map are useful aids, but they are not interchangeable with direct aircraft attitude awareness.

Before a simulator exercise, agree a starting state, exercise boundary, instructor intervention signal and stop condition. Afterwards compare intended movement with actual movement. Record one observation, its possible cause and the next controlled practice step. Better practice reduces uncertainty instead of rewarding a lucky recovery.

## Desk exercise

In a simulator, annotate three screenshots showing roll, pitch and yaw. Explain one difference between aircraft-relative and pilot-relative directions.

## Marking guide

Award credit for correct axes, separation of attitude and position, and a defined stop condition. Do not assess physical flight competence from screenshots.
