# Lesson 1: Keep pilot preparation separate from permission to operate

Course: SACAA Drone Pilot License Preparation

Status: DRAFT. Original SkyScholar authoring; technical and instructional review pending.

Estimated study time: 20-30 minutes including the desk exercise (author estimate).

## Learning focus

Identify the current South African authority and training references.

## Lesson

This is a draft South African preparation pathway. Begin with current SACAA material and an appropriately authorised training provider. The course title is retained from the historical catalogue; terminology, syllabus coverage and licensing requirements must be reconciled with current official sources before release.

Separate the person, the aircraft and the organisation operating it. A pilot qualification concerns the person. Aircraft registration or other applicable aircraft requirements concern the equipment. Operational authorisations concern the activity and operator. Meeting one requirement does not automatically satisfy the others. Landowner agreement, where relevant, also does not replace aviation requirements.

The current SkyScholar software has seven provisional subjects: air law, aircraft knowledge, principles of flight, meteorology, navigation, human factors and communications. These are organisational starting points. They are not a declaration that the complete current SACAA syllabus has been mapped or that the software's quiz duration and target match an official exam.

Build a mapping table for each objective: official source, edition, section, author note, reviewer and status. Leave unverified cells visibly unresolved. An instructor should examine whether the lesson teaches the objective, whether scenarios are locally appropriate and whether distractors introduce ambiguity.

For the exercise, work only with a fictional proposed operation. Identify which questions concern the pilot, aircraft, operator and site. The result should be a research and review plan, not a claim that the operation is approved. No aviation course becomes public merely because its files have been authored.
