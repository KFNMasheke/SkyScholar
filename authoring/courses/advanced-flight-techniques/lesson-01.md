# Lesson 1: Increase complexity one variable at a time

Course: Advanced Flight Techniques

Status: DRAFT. Original SkyScholar authoring; technical and instructional review pending.

Estimated study time: 20-30 minutes including the desk exercise (author estimate).

## Learning focus

Build a simulator progression from simple to complex tasks.

## Lesson

Advanced performance is repeatable decision-making under a defined workload. It is not a collection of dramatic manoeuvres. Begin with a simulator baseline that the learner can complete consistently: stable orientation, a simple path and a deliberate stop. Document the conditions so the next exercise is comparable.

Increase only one demand at a time. A route can become more complex, a camera task can be introduced, or a simulated wind condition can change. Changing everything together prevents the instructor from identifying why performance deteriorated. A successful outcome after an uncontrolled recovery is not evidence of a reliable method.

Describe margins explicitly. Aircraft performance, attention, available space and time to respond are finite. A task that uses nearly all attention for framing may leave little capacity for monitoring warnings. More automation can change workload rather than simply reducing it. The crew still needs to understand what mode is active and what the aircraft will do when the plan changes.

For each exercise, define a normal completion criterion and an interruption criterion. The learner should be able to explain when to stop without treating that decision as failure. Record errors by observable behaviour: delayed recognition, incorrect mode assumption or an incomplete cross-check. Avoid vague judgements such as bad flying.

A progression plan should end with an instructor review of evidence, not a self-issued operational qualification. Real aircraft exercises require the appropriate training environment, instructor, permissions and aircraft-specific procedures. This opening lesson remains a desk and simulator activity.

## Desk exercise

Design three simulator exercises that add one demand each. Include a stop condition and a measurable debrief question for every exercise.

## Marking guide

Credit controlled progression and measurable behaviour. Reject plans that rely on unapproved real-world proximity flying or treat a simulator result as operational authorisation.
