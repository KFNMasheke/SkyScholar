# Lesson 1: Preserve the evidence before changing the system

Course: Drone Maintenance and Troubleshooting

Status: DRAFT. Original SkyScholar authoring; technical and instructional review pending.

Estimated study time: 20-30 minutes including the desk exercise (author estimate).

## Learning focus

Separate a symptom from a diagnosis.

## Lesson

A team reports intermittent vibration after replacing a propeller. The symptom is vibration under particular conditions. The recent replacement is relevant evidence, but it is not yet proof of the cause. Begin by recording the aircraft identification, configuration, part details, time of the change and when the symptom first appeared.

Follow the aircraft manufacturer's inspection and maintenance instructions. Do not improvise a live flight to reproduce an unexplained fault. A damaged or suspect propulsion component requires a safe stop and an appropriate technical review. Power down and isolate equipment according to its instructions before handling it. Battery damage, heat or swelling must be managed using the applicable manufacturer and site procedures.

Good troubleshooting changes one controlled variable at a time and states the expected observation. Record what was inspected, what was changed, who did it and what the result was. A software update, calibration or restart can alter the state and erase clues. Capture permitted diagnostic records first, without collecting unnecessary personal information.

Use a fault log with separate columns for symptom, evidence, hypothesis, test and result. A hypothesis can be rejected; that is useful progress. Replacing several parts together may restore operation but leave the cause unknown. Record that uncertainty honestly.

Return to service is a defined process with competent oversight, required checks and a record of release. This lesson teaches documentation and decision structure. It does not authorise repairs or override manufacturer limits.

## Desk exercise

Complete a fictional fault log for vibration after a component change, identifying three useful records and two unsupported assumptions to avoid.

## Marking guide

A good log preserves configuration, separates cause from timing, uses approved instructions and states who may release the aircraft back to service.

## Reflection

Name one assumption you would verify before applying this lesson to a real project. Identify the source or competent person who could resolve it.
