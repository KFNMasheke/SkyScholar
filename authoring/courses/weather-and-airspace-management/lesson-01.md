# Lesson 1: Information is useful only within its place and time

Course: Weather and Airspace Management

Status: DRAFT. Original SkyScholar authoring; technical and instructional review pending.

Estimated study time: 20-30 minutes including the desk exercise (author estimate).

## Learning focus

Distinguish forecast, observation and site evidence.

## Lesson

A weather forecast is a prediction for a place and period. An observation describes conditions at its reporting point and time. A site observation describes what the crew can currently see or measure nearby. These sources complement each other, but none automatically describes every part of the operating area.

Consider a site beside a ridge and large buildings. A regional wind forecast may not capture the local flow around those features. Note the source, issue time, valid period and location for each item. Then record where its applicability is uncertain. A gap in information should remain visible in the plan rather than being filled with a favourable assumption.

Airspace information also has boundaries and validity periods. A screenshot copied from a previous job can look authoritative while being unsuitable for today's place, time or activity. Establish a current information source, the applicable jurisdiction, the operating area and the relevant notices or restrictions. A map layer is a tool for interpretation, not permission by itself.

Combine the evidence with aircraft limitations and the operation's approved procedures. Define when the crew will reassess and what conditions require stopping. Avoid converting an illustrative classroom wind value or distance into a universal operating rule.

For the exercise, write a decision record rather than a weather prediction. Include the evidence used, the unresolved questions, who can resolve them, and the point at which the mission must be postponed. An explicit no-go can be the best supported professional outcome.

## Desk exercise

Compare three fictional information records with different issue times and coverage. Decide which questions remain unresolved before a mission can proceed.

## Marking guide

The answer should distinguish issue time from valid time, identify location gaps and avoid treating a forecast or airspace screenshot as permission.

## Reflection

Name one assumption you would verify before applying this lesson to a real project. Identify the source or competent person who could resolve it.
