# Lesson 1: The aircraft is only one part of the system

Course: Introduction to Drone Technology

Status: DRAFT. Original SkyScholar authoring; technical and instructional review pending.

Estimated study time: 20-30 minutes including the desk exercise (author estimate).

## Learning focus

Identify the aircraft, control station, command link and payload.

## Lesson

A client asks for a map of a small construction site. Before choosing a drone, write down the result the client needs: a dated visual record, a measurable surface, or an inspection finding. These are different products. A sharp photograph does not automatically provide reliable measurements, and a model that looks complete can still contain gaps.

Think in four connected parts. The aircraft carries the equipment. The remote pilot station allows the crew to monitor and direct it. The command link carries instructions and status. The payload collects the useful information. The people, procedures and operating environment determine whether these parts can work together safely. A camera specification alone cannot describe mission capability.

A multirotor can hold position and inspect a small area from different directions, but carrying more mass affects its performance. A fixed-wing aircraft generally suits sustained forward travel; its launch, recovery and turning needs must fit the site. A hybrid combines operating modes with additional system complexity. These are selection considerations, not promises about a particular model.

Separate three questions in every equipment comparison: Can it collect the required information? Can the crew operate it within the relevant limitations and permissions? Can the team check and deliver usable results? Automation can help execute a planned path. It cannot establish that the path is safe, lawful, or suitable for the data product.

For the construction request, produce a short equipment decision with assumptions. If the client has not defined accuracy, inspection criteria, or output format, record those as questions rather than selecting the most expensive aircraft.

## Desk exercise

Create a four-part system diagram for a fictional construction survey. Add one failure consequence and one verification step for each part.

## Marking guide

A strong answer links aircraft, control station, command link and payload to the client deliverable. It distinguishes capture capability from permission and validation, and identifies missing client requirements.

## Reflection

Name one assumption you would verify before applying this lesson to a real project. Identify the source or competent person who could resolve it.
