# Communications: opening lesson

Status: DRAFT. Provisional South African subject; current syllabus mapping and competent review pending.

## Objective

Resolve ambiguity before acting.

## Lesson

Effective team communication makes the speaker, recipient, intended action and important conditions clear. In this desk lesson, use fictional crew roles and written messages. Operational radio phraseology, licensing and frequencies require current local references and competent instruction before practical use.

Ambiguity can arise from similar names, missing units, an unclear recipient or an unstated change of plan. A receiver should clarify uncertainty rather than choosing the most convenient interpretation. A response should demonstrate that the intended meaning was understood, not merely that a sound was heard.

Closed-loop communication includes a clear instruction or information item, an appropriate acknowledgement or readback, and confirmation or correction where needed. It is especially useful when a misunderstanding would have a material consequence. The exact required form depends on the approved procedure and context.

Keep operational records factual and proportionate. Record relevant changes and decisions without adding unnecessary personal information. After an unclear exchange, review the message structure and the team's process. Do not turn this general classroom model into a claim that a particular phrase is approved for live aviation radio use.
