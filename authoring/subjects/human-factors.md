# Human factors: opening lesson

Status: DRAFT. Provisional South African subject; current syllabus mapping and competent review pending.

## Objective

Make pressure and uncertainty visible in decisions.

## Lesson

Human performance is part of the operating system. Workload, fatigue, attention and expectations affect how people interpret information and respond. A technically available aircraft does not establish that the crew is ready for the task.

External pressure can narrow attention. A client deadline, travel cost or desire to finish may encourage a person to dismiss contradictory evidence. Record the evidence and decision criteria before debating the commercial inconvenience of a delay. A stop decision supported by evidence can be a successful professional outcome.

Checklists support consistency, but reading words without checking the actual condition does not complete the purpose of a check. Clear role allocation and a shared understanding of intervention criteria help a team detect an omitted step or a mistaken assumption.

Use a debrief that separates observations from blame. Describe what happened, what information was available, what the team assumed and what process should change. Avoid inferring a person's medical condition or competence from a single error. The aim is a better decision system, with competent review where individual readiness is uncertain.
