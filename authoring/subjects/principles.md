# Principles of flight: opening lesson

Status: DRAFT. Provisional South African subject; current syllabus mapping and competent review pending.

## Objective

Reason about forces with stated assumptions.

## Lesson

Use a simplified multirotor model for this desk lesson. Weight acts downward. The aircraft's total thrust has a direction and magnitude. In a stationary level hover without other significant vertical effects, its upward component balances weight. This is an explanatory model, not a universal aircraft control setting.

When the aircraft tilts, the thrust vector tilts. Its vertical component becomes smaller if the total magnitude stays the same, while a horizontal component is introduced. Maintaining height while accelerating horizontally therefore requires an appropriate change in the total force balance. The flight controller's implementation depends on the aircraft.

Distinguish attitude, velocity and acceleration. Attitude is orientation; velocity describes motion; acceleration describes a change in velocity. A change in attitude need not produce the same immediate displacement in every environment. Wind and control response matter.

State assumptions before doing calculations. A diagram should name the aircraft type, axes, force directions and any effects omitted. Use the result to explain a concept, not to prescribe an unreviewed flight manoeuvre. Aircraft-specific performance and practical training belong in later reviewed lessons.
